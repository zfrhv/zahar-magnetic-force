If there is no a in there, then the result will just go to 0. $$W = - a \int_{0}^{t} F_T \cdot t \cdot dt + \frac{1}{2}mv^2$$ We can receive \\(\frac{1}{2}mv^2\\) easily back when we are at infinity, so it is not the energy that we have spent into the potential energy thus we can ignore it. $$\Delta E_p = - a \int_{0}^{t} F_T \cdot t \cdot dt$$ Lets separate our force: $$F_T = force that depends on a + force that doesnt depends on a = F(a) + F(not a)$$ $$\Delta E_p = - a \int_{0}^{t} (F(a) \cdot t + F(not\ a) \cdot t) \cdot dt$$ As mentioned before we have \\(t \to 0\\) so anything that doesnt has a will simply go to 0: $$\Delta E_p = - a \int_{0}^{t} F(a) \cdot t \cdot dt$$ Our a is constant so we can bring F(a) out: $$\Delta E_p = - a \cdot F(a)\int_{0}^{t} t \cdot dt$$ Lets assume that we started from v=0 for simplicity: $$\frac{-\frac{1}{2} \Delta v_r^2 + \Delta v_{r_\bot}^2}{r} - \frac{-\frac{1}{2} 0^2 + 0^2}{r} = - a \cdot F(a) \cdot \frac{t^2}{2}$$ $$\frac{-\frac{1}{2} \Delta v_r^2 + \Delta v_{r_\bot}^2}{r} = - a \cdot F(a) \cdot \frac{t^2}{2}$$ $$F(a) = a \cdot ?$$ $$\frac{-\frac{1}{2} \Delta v_r^2 + \Delta v_{r_\bot}^2}{r} = - a \cdot a \cdot ? \cdot \frac{t^2}{2} = - ? \cdot \frac{v^2}{2}$$ TODO idk And the direction of the force is in the direction of the movement: $$F(a) = TODO...\hat a$$ but ofc we dont wanna violate the angular momentum so ...
