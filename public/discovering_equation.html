Discovering process
The techniqe of discovering was: - No clue what to do - Take random guess - See why the guess is wrong - Understand the mistake and make a new guess having better knowladge My first problem was that old method violates third newtons law, so I started guessing force that depends on the degrees between the speeds. Realized that the speeds must be relative, so force between 2 particles can depend only on the speed between those 2 particles. Thus \\(\Delta v\\) must be used. Judging by logic and units I needed \\(\Delta v^2\\) and not just \\(\Delta v\\), so I tried to find a force but nothing was working. I realizsed that I can't ignore the protons charges, and that they also create a force same as the electrons. Did a quick check whether \\(F = \frac{\Delta v^2}{r^2}\\) flips when we flip the speed of electron and it was a success. Since then (around 1-2 years passed) I knew that there is a better force function. I started a journey to find what is the speed + charges inside the wires and how do they change when resistance is different. (just wasted 1 year) I wrote a program in python for manual integration and started guessing forces that give exactly same result as the old method. After another 1-2 years I found the weird force \\(\frac{v^2 - v_r^2}{r^2}\\) but it was violating energy, and when playing with the code I found out that there should be another \\(F \thickapprox a/r\\) so I realized that everything is nonesense and threw it away. I began on looking on wire part as a whole ignoring the separation between protons and electrons. There I realized that I should work with energy instead of forces, and that i can derivtate the force from energy thus not violating anything. I found perfect for equation for wire parts but it wasnt working for changing current. Then I realized when the wire is moving all he gets is extra speed, so the force must depend on the speed, so I started over again. I found another force \\(\frac{\frac{1}{2}v_r^2 - v_{r_\bot}^2}{r^2}\\) and realized its the same as before (simple pythagoras), hinting that this is the only solution thats there. This time I started guessing from energy perspective, and in order to not violate energy I realized that there must be force that depends on the acceleration. And so I found this final result that exactly solves the wire parts forces (not freely moving charges).
